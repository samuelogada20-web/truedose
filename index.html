Learn

Short guides for safer buying and use.

Signs a pack may be fake
  • Spelling mistakes, blurry print or colours that look off.
  • No registration number, or a number that belongs to a different product.
  • Missing or smudged batch number and expiry date.
  • Damaged, resealed or loose packaging.
  • Tablets that differ in colour, shape or smell from your usual pack.
  • A price far below other pharmacies.
How the scratch-code check works

Some products, mainly antimalarials and certain antibiotics, carry a scratch panel. Scratch it at the point of purchase, then text the code to the short code printed on the pack. The reply tells you whether the pack is genuine or suspicious.

Buying medicine safely
  • Buy from a registered pharmacy that displays its licence.
  • Avoid hawkers, buses and open markets.
  • Ask the pharmacist for the brand, strength and how to take it.
  • Keep the pack and receipt until the course is finished.
Storing medicines at home
  • Keep them in the original pack, in a cool, dry place away from direct sun.
  • Keep them out of reach of children.
  • Check expiry dates regularly and do not use expired medicines.
  • Do not share prescription medicines with other people.
If you have already taken a suspect medicine

Stop using it and keep the pack. If you feel unwell, speak to a doctor or pharmacist, and get emergency care for severe symptoms. Then report the product to NAFDAC.

About TrueDose

A simple way to check medicines in Nigeria.

What it does

TrueDose guides you through checking a medicine against NAFDAC’s official Greenbook, helps you find pharmacies, and makes it easy to report suspected fakes.

What it does not do

TrueDose is not a lab test and cannot confirm a pack is genuine on its own. It is not affiliated with NAFDAC and does not give medical advice.
